A portfolio is a small system with a surprisingly tricky brief: make the work easy to understand, and leave room for the person behind it.

This site is the current experiment. The visual direction pairs editorial typography and generous space with handwritten margin notes and a cursor-following portrait.

## The constraints

The site has three primary pages: Home, Projects, and Writing. It should work with a small amount of real content, stay readable on a phone, and make professional experience easy to find.

The portrait is currently a placeholder illustration. It is not a likeness; the face asset and eye layer are separate so personal artwork can replace it later.

## A deliberately small architecture

Next.js App Router renders the pages. TypeScript defines the content shape. Project case studies and writing live in MDX files, separate from their layouts. A static export keeps deployment simple.

Only the navigation state and portrait need client-side behavior. The lists, article content, and page layouts render without requiring a client content system.

## Making eye contact

Each pupil measures the pointer relative to its own eye center. Its movement is constrained to an ellipse and clipped to its socket. A short interpolation lets it settle into position rather than snapping on every pointer event.

Animation frames stop when the eyes settle. The portrait resets when the pointer leaves the window, and tracking is paused when it is offscreen. A keyboard-accessible control pauses the interaction. Reduced-motion preferences disable it by default.

## Trade-offs

SVG pupils provide continuous movement with a small asset footprint. A directional sprite sheet could better match pixel artwork, but would require aligning and maintaining separate gaze frames. The current layered structure leaves that option open.

The site's limited content is visible rather than disguised. There are no fabricated client projects or invented performance numbers.

## What is still evolving

Personal portrait assets, detailed career history, and published engineering notes will make this more personal. Accessibility and layout checks are part of implementation; this page makes no claim about measured production performance yet.

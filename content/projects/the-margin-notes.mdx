This is my current portfolio. It uses editorial typography, handwritten margin notes, and a portrait whose eyes follow the cursor.

## The constraints

The site has three primary pages: Home, Projects, and Writing. It should work with a small amount of real content, stay readable on a phone, and make professional experience easy to find.

The portrait uses supplied personal artwork. The eye-free base and directional eye frames are separate, so the interaction can change without redrawing the face.

## Architecture

Next.js App Router renders the pages. TypeScript defines the content shape. Project case studies and writing live in MDX files, separate from their layouts. A static export keeps deployment simple.

<Architecture />

Only the navigation state and portrait need client-side behavior. The lists, article content, and page layouts render without requiring a client content system.

## Making eye contact

The pointer is measured relative to the portrait, then mapped to one of eight directional eye frames. A cross-eyed frame appears when the pointer is near the nose. Each eye uses its own fixed placement box, with size and inter-eye spacing configured independently.

Frames are scheduled only when the pointer or layout changes. The portrait resets when the pointer leaves the window, and tracking pauses offscreen. The eyes return to neutral after two seconds without pointer movement. Reduced-motion preferences disable tracking.

## Trade-offs

Directional sprites preserve the style of the supplied portrait. They require careful alignment and switch between discrete directions rather than continuous pupil motion. The base image and eye layers remain separate, with soft SVG masks to blend the cutout edges.

## What is still evolving

I’m adding engineering notes and personal projects as I work on them. I check accessibility and layout during implementation, but haven’t measured production performance yet.
